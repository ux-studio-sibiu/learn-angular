Lesson 08

Forms

Angular has two built-in form systems. Reactive forms define the form model and its rules in TypeScript, and the template only binds to it. Template-driven forms put the rules in HTML attributes. Both track value, validity and interaction state for you.

  • Click Register with an empty form: markAllAsTouched() reveals every error at once, like ModelState errors after a POST.
  • Unsaved changes: type something, then click another lesson in the nav. The route's canDeactivate guard asks you to confirm. After a successful submit, the form is pristine again and you can leave freely.
  • Typed forms: form.getRawValue().guests is a number, and a typo like form.controls.emial is a compile error.

Template-driven feels like jQuery Validate (rules in markup), but it gets hard to test and reason about as forms grow. Reactive forms are the default for anything with real validation, which is what you'll see in most codebases.

Good to know

  • Validators are plain functions that return null or an error object. Custom ones are a few lines (see ticketCode), and group-level ones handle rules that span several fields.
  • updateOn: 'blur' validates when you leave the field, which keeps async checks from firing on every keystroke.
  • Wrapping a custom component (like the lesson 04 stepper) as a form control means implementing ControlValueAccessor. That's the Angular way to make design-system inputs work with forms.
  • Signal-based forms are experimental in newer Angular versions (v21+). In v20 and in existing code, reactive forms with Observables are the standard.
